What the model shows

  • Only the bay 4 cage is left, holding two drives right under the radiator fans; the dashed outline is where the bay 3 cage was. Bays 1 to 3 are open, so the tunnel has a clear run back to both x16 slots.
  • The V100 sits in PCIEX16_1 at PCIe 3.0 x16. Its end is 131 mm behind the front steel, and that’s the room the tunnel has.
  • One tunnel, front to back: the Lian Li on the inside of the steel, screwed to straightener A (the square v2 you printed), the NZXT, then twin funnel B to collar C on the card end. The middle and bottom stock fans come out.
  • The tunnel is centered between the two x16 slots, so card 1 sits about 30 mm above the fan’s center and card 2 about 30 mm below. Until card 2 arrives, cap D plugs its socket so all the air goes to card 1.
  • That center is about 60 mm below the middle fan holes, right across the strip of steel between the middle and bottom holes, so fan 1 breathes through both, about three quarters of its face. A front adapter on the case’s own screw holes will hold it, so nothing gets drilled.
  • Two fans push in series. The straightener takes the swirl out between the Lian Li and the NZXT, so the NZXT adds closer to its full pressure.
  • With both cards in, each half of the fan blows almost straight into its own card, and nothing reaches the PSU shroud.
  • The AIO tubes now leave the pump upward, over the RAM, so nothing hangs across PCIEX16_1.
  • The FENVI 2.5 GbE card stays in PCIEX1_1, just above the V100. Its heatsink hangs a few millimeters over the card’s backplate. It fits, but it gets little air, so watch its temperature.
  • The Wi-Fi card is in PCIEX16_3, the bottom slot. A second V100 in PCIEX16_2 covers the x1 slot below it and leaves the Wi-Fi card about 5 mm under its shroud. With two V100s, PCIEX1_1 and PCIEX16_3 are the best spots for the two small cards.
