  • The duct is one piece: flange, loft and the sleeve over the card end. The funnel, collar and cap of v2, and the taped joints between them, are gone.
  • Nothing screws to the card. The sleeve slips 10 mm over the card end with 0.15 mm to spare, and a thin lip at its back edge squeezes the card all round, so it seals without being forced on.
  • The front is screwed, like now: your adapter hangs on two screws through the middle fan’s top holes, and four fan screws hold fan 1 to it. Its slots let it slide 4 mm up or down to meet the duct.

Tunnel parts

    How it goes together

    1. On the bench: fan 1 onto your adapter with its four fan screws from the plate’s front, as it is now.
    2. On the bench: rail frame on fan 2’s back, then fan 2 and the straightener. Push the four pins in from the rail frame side until their arrowheads poke out of the straightener, then press the lot onto fan 1’s back until all four click.
    3. Front panel off: hang the stack on two fan screws through the middle fan’s top holes, from the outside. Leave them loose.
    4. On the bench: plug the EPS lead into the card, then push the duct’s sleeve onto the card end. The thin lip bends and wipes round the card; it goes on by hand.
    5. Lay the case on its side and lower the card into its slot. The duct’s flange drops into the rail frame from the glass side and slides down with the card; the rail’s lead-in lifts or lowers the loose stack into line.
    6. Tighten the two screws. The lead lies in the trough and sweeps up over the stack; park the EPS adapter on top and plug in the two PCIe leads.

    Check at the fit test

    1. The snap test is in: pin 1 (0.30 mm barb) in the 4.4 mm hole fit best, the 4.2 mm hole was too small, the rail clicked in easily and the lip was springy. v4 uses all of that.pins: pin 1 · holes 4.4, and 4.6 through the 20 mm straightener
    2. One pin first: push it through fan 2 and a straightener corner into fan 1. Its arrowhead should click just behind fan 1’s back flange, in the open corner. If it won’t click or it rattles, only the pin length changes, a six-minute print.model: 4 mm flange, 0.2 mm slack
    3. The tray side. Your adapter already fits there, and the rail frame and the duct’s flange are no wider, 124 mm, so they should clear too.model: 2 mm
    4. The height. With the card in, the flange should enter the rail without forcing. If it doesn’t, loosen the two screws and let the stack slide in its slots.model: case holes 3 mm below the slots’ centres, ±4 to spare
    5. The lip should push on with light force and touch the card all round. If it’s too stiff, it can be printed thinner.model: 0.6 mm squeeze, 3 layers
    6. The flange should slide in by hand and sit flat on the rail frame.model: 0.3 mm dovetail clearance
    7. The gap between the trough’s walls and the card end. A strip of 3 mm foam or tape closes it if air gets out there.model: 1.5 mm
    8. First power-on only with the EPS plug in and both fans running. Watch the card’s temperature with nvidia-smi.
